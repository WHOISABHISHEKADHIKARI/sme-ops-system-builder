# Backlink & Citation Directory Master List

A tiered working list. Tier 0 and Tier 1 are where a small business should spend its
effort. The lower tiers are useful in small numbers, and the bottom tier is the list most
"free high DA backlinks" articles are really about - the one to read so it can be avoided.

**The rule that outranks every other rule on this page:** a directory link is worth
something only if the listing is real, the directory is genuinely read by customers, and
the same NAP appears on it. A listing nobody visits passes no value. A fabricated listing
is a liability that can be reported and removed, and in a competitive local market it is
worse than having no listing at all.

---

## Before any submission - the four checks

Run these on every directory before spending 15 minutes on it. They take two minutes.

| Check | How | Pass looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Is the domain actually authoritative? | Free Moz Link Explorer (10 queries/mo) and Ahrefs Webmaster Tools backlink checker | DR 40+ from a site with real referring domains |
| Does the directory's own backlink profile look real? | Same tools - check its referring domain count and follow/nofollow mix | Ref domains in the hundreds or thousands, not a handful of PBNs |
| Does it pass link to competitors with a real editorial site? | Search `[category] + site:directory.com` | Competitors are agencies with real sites, not other directory listings |
| What is the link type and the approval time? | Read the submit page | Nofollow is fine. Instant approval means no review, and no review means no value |

**Kill criteria - walk away if any of these are true:**

- It demands a reciprocal backlink for a free listing. That is a link scheme and it
  violates Google's link spam policies.
- It asks for a keyword-stuffed business name. Keyword-stuffing the name is a
  violation of Google's business name policy and can get the profile suspended.
- Every other listing is a link to another directory, an article with 300 links, or a
  "write a guest post" page selling a post back to you.
- The approval is instant, and there is no editor.
- The page is behind a wall of adSense and its own outbound links.

## Tier 7 - PR and newswire

A press release is not news. A free newswire distribution creates a low-value page on
every small press-release site at once, which is a pattern Google has targeted directly.

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Free newswire services (wires, press portals with instant approval) | Skip. The pages are unindexed or thin and the pattern is a known spam signal |
| A real local news story | Pursue this. One genuine story in a real local paper is the strongest local link available |
| HARO / Connectively (now Cision) | Being quoted as an expert in a real publication - a legitimate earned link |
| Guest post on a real industry site with real traffic | The best white-hat link available. Editorial, not a directory |
| Broken-link building | Find a broken link on a relevant page and offer the replacement. Works, and it is honest |
| Unlinked mention reclaiming | Find mentions without links and ask for one |
| Podcast guest appearances | The host's show page links to the guest. Real, editorial, durable |
| Association and community newsletters | A member update with a link |

## Tier 8 - The list to refuse

Every "165 free high-DA directory submission sites" article lists a recognisable group of
sites. They share design, spelling, and a submission flow that asks for a keyword field
with no reason for it. The examples below are named so they can be recognised and
skipped, not so they can be submitted to.

**What they have in common:**

- DA 50-70 but almost no real referring domains
- Nofollow-free "dofollow base" language in the pitch
- Approval instantly, with no editorial review
- A "write a review" or "post a press release" page that is really a paid link
- Outbound links to other sites in the same network
- They have all been the subject of Google link-scheme and network-crackdown action

**Why it backfires.** Google has run manual actions and algorithmic demotions against link
networks for over a decade. A network-wide footprint on a small local domain can cost the
site more than any gain. The listing is on a site whose only asset is selling links, and
it will be gone the next time that site is deindexed.

**If one of these is already submitted:** leave it. It is inert at worst. Do not build more
of them, and do not link to one from one of Tier 1.

## The NAP block - copy it, never retype it

Every listing in every tier carries the same five values. Get them right once.

| Element | Rule | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Business name | The registered or trading name, in the same order, every time | "Acme Dental" in one place, "Dr. Acme Dental Clinic" in another |
| Address | As the postal format of the country requires, suite/floor included, matching the signage and the GBP entry | One place uses "4th Fl", another "4th Floor, Unit 402" |
| Phone | One number. The same one. In international format where the platform wants it | An old landline still live on three directories |
| Website | The canonical domain, https, no tracking parameters, no `www` variation between listings | `yoursite.com` on some, `https://www.yoursite.com/` on others |
| Category | The primary category, worded as Google words it | "Dental Clinic" versus "Dentist" versus "Cosmetic Dentist" |

**The procedure that works:**

1. Write the canonical NAP block into a single file, as plain text, with no formatting.
2. Paste it into every platform. Do not retype. Do not "improve" it.
3. When the address genuinely changes, change it everywhere in one sitting, starting with
   the website, then GBP, then Bing and Apple, then the directories.
4. Re-check the top ten listings quarterly. Directories decay silently.
5. Never add a keyword to the business name, on a profile or a directory. If the name
   needs a keyword, the answer is a permitted marketing description, not a fake DBA.

## The order to do it in

| Week | Action | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Site live, canonical NAP, one page per service | Everything links here |
| 1 | Google Business Profile claimed and verified | The map pack is the local ranking asset |
| 2 | Bing Places, Apple Business Connect | Same data, two more surfaces |
| 3 | LinkedIn Company Page completed, Crunchbase profile | Highest-authority, lowest-effort citations |
| 4 | Review platforms relevant to the industry: one of G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Yelp, TrustRadius | Real buyer traffic |
| 5 | Chamber of commerce, council directory, trade association | The genuinely local links, and the ones competitors skip |
| 6 | Two or three niche vertical directories | Relevance over authority |
| 7 | One guest contribution or one real local PR angle | The only link that compounds |
| 8 onwards | One Tier 2 bookmark a week, one review a week, one GBP post a week | Cadence beats volume |

**The review cadence is the one that actually moves rank.** One new review a week, replied
to, from real customers, forever, outperforms any directory list on this page. Google weighs
review count, recency and rating together - and the replies to them.
